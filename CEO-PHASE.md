# CEO Phase — Design Document

*The ultimate do-nothing job. The punchline to the entire game.*

---

## Core Concept

The game's progression is: **worker → manager → executive → useless figurehead**.

- Early game: you ARE the company, clicking everything
- Mid game: you hire people to click for you (CTO, COO, CFO, VP of Ops)
- Late game: you hire a CEO and the Operations tab transforms into a CEO Dashboard
- You literally can't see the spreadsheet anymore. You're too important for that.
- Each CEO action affects revenue/stock indirectly
- Want to actually do real work (tweak budgets, view P&L)? Costs RE — you're "disrupting the org chart"
- Stock price becomes THE score. Cash and revenue are handled by your team.
- **Golden Parachute = prestige bridge.** Build your exit package → cash out → new game+.

## The RE Gate

Want to access Operations? That costs RE now.

| Action | RE Cost | Notes |
|--------|---------|-------|
| View P&L / Operations tab | 25 RE | "Getting in the weeds" |
| Adjust CTO/COO budgets | 50 RE | "Micromanaging" |
| Manually hire someone | 100 RE | "Undermining the COO" |
| Fire someone | 75 RE | "Use the layoffs button instead" |
| Change tax strategy | 50 RE | "That's what the CPA is for" |

The CEO is so detached that doing actual work costs resources. Forces you to trust your automation.

## CEO Actions — Complete Catalog

### 🪑 THE MUNDANE (what CEOs actually do all day)

**Schedule a Meeting**
- No effect. Just burns time (cooldown).
- Small random chance of something useful happening (~5%).
- Harvard study: CEOs spend 72% of work time in meetings.
- Flavor text: "You met with the leadership team to discuss alignment on cross-functional synergies."

**All-Hands Town Hall**
- Give a speech to employees. Multiple choice talking points.
- Morale boost (temporary revenue bump) or morale tank (wrong talking points).
- Choices: "Celebrate wins" (safe, small bump), "Announce changes" (risky, bigger swing), "Ramble about vision" (random).

**Strategy Offsite**
- "Take the leadership team to a resort to align on Q3 priorities."
- Costs cash, does nothing measurable.
- Slight random stock bump because Wall Street likes "strategic direction."
- Diminishing returns — too many offsites = analyst concern about "lack of focus."

**Read the WSJ**
- Passive action. Small chance of spotting a market trend (temporary bonus).
- Mostly flavor. "You read about a competitor's struggles. Feeling good."

**Approve Expense Reports**
- The one actual operational task.
- Costs RE because you're "getting involved in operations."
- Small cash savings. Mostly a joke.

---

### 📱 SOCIAL MEDIA / PR (the attention economy)

**Post on X**
- Random templates ranging from corporate pablum to unhinged takes.
- Stock impact varies:
  - Boring corporate speak = small bump (+1-2%)
  - Controversial hot take = could go either way (-5% to +10%)
  - Outright stupid = tank (-10-20%)
- Template categories: Inspirational, Corporate, Controversial, Unhinged
- Cooldown: ~30s
- Inspired by: Musk's "funding secured" $420 tweet → SEC lawsuit

**CNBC Interview**
- Analyst questions, multiple choice answers.
- Bullish promises boost short-term stock but set dangerous expectations for next earnings.
- Humble answers are "boring" but safe.
- Deflecting = small negative, analysts hate non-answers.
- 3-4 questions per interview.

**Keynote Speech**
- TED-talk style. Pick a buzzword to center it around.
- Buzzword options: AI, Blockchain, Sustainability, Metaverse, Quantum, Web3, etc.
- **Trend alignment matters** — picking the hot buzzword = stock bump. Yesterday's buzzword = stock drop.
- Rotating trends keep it fresh (trends shift every few quarters).
- Costs RE to organize.

**Company Blog Post**
- Write a "leadership thought piece."
- Small positive sentiment.
- Diminishing returns if you spam it.
- Flavor: "You published '7 Leadership Lessons I Learned From My Golden Retriever' on LinkedIn."

### 🪓 THE RUTHLESS (Wall Street loves this stuff)

**Announce Layoffs**
- Fires employees across departments (COO has to rehire them).
- Short-term stock bump (analysts love "efficiency").
- Long-term revenue drop (fewer employees = less output).
- The irony of undoing your own automation chain.
- Multiple scales: "Trim" (10%), "Restructure" (25%), "Bloodbath" (50%).
- Real: every major tech company 2022-2024.

**Stock Buyback**
- Spend company cash to inflate stock price directly.
- Pure stock manipulation dressed up as "returning value to shareholders."
- Scales with amount spent. Diminishing returns per dollar.
- Real: companies spend hundreds of billions annually on this.

**Spin Off Division**
- Sell off a revenue source for a big cash lump + stock bump.
- You literally lose a department permanently.
- Trade-off: short-term gain vs long-term revenue loss.
- Real: every conglomerate breakup ever.

**Acquire a Competitor**
- Spend massive cash. Random outcome:
  - Successful integration: +new revenue source, stock bump
  - Culture clash: -revenue temporarily, flat stock
  - Total disaster: write-down, stock tank, cash wasted
- Probability shifts based on how many you've done (more acquisitions = higher disaster chance).
- Real: HP/Compaq, FIS/Worldpay ($42B → sold for half), AOL/Time Warner.

### 💰 THE SELF-DEALING (peak satire)

**Vote Yourself a Raise**
- Increase CEO compensation package. Drains cash, does nothing for the company.
- Adds to personal "CEO Score" / golden parachute value.
- Board might push back at extreme levels (requires more RE to force through).
- Real: CEO-to-worker pay ratio is 399:1.

**Renovate the Corner Office**
- Vanity spend. Each renovation more absurd than the last:
  - Level 1: Standing desk + ergonomic chair ($50K)
  - Level 2: Personal barista station ($200K)
  - Level 3: Indoor putting green ($500K)
  - Level 4: Meditation pod with waterfall ($1M)
  - Level 5: Reclaimed barn wood everything ($5M)
  - Level 6: "My soul can't breathe" complete redo ($10M)
- Each level: tiny stock bump (analysts like "executive investment"), big cash drain.
- Real: WeWork's Rebekah Neumann had her office redone 6 times.

**Corporate Jet**
- Tiered purchase:
  - Level 1: Timeshare on a jet ($2M/yr)
  - Level 2: Own jet ($50M + maintenance)
  - Level 3: Fleet of jets ($200M)
- Occasionally useful (faster travel for deals, slightly better acquisition odds).
- Mostly just flex. Adds to golden parachute value.
- Real: every Fortune 500 jet controversy.

**Golden Parachute** ⭐ KEY MECHANIC
- Structure your exit package.
- Invest cash/RE into your parachute at any time.
- The bigger it is, the bigger your new game+ multiplier when you "cash out."
- This IS the prestige bridge:
  - Parachute value determines: starting cash multiplier, RE multiplier, unlock speed
  - "Cash Out" button appears once parachute ≥ threshold
  - The whole CEO phase is about optimizing your exit before you prestige
- Real: Marissa Mayer walked with $187M from Yahoo.

### 🎰 THE WTF (inspired by real headlines)

**"Funding Secured" Tweet**
- Post something implying a major deal that doesn't exist.
- Huge stock spike (+20-40%), then SEC investigation after delay.
- SEC investigation: large fine (cash), forced to step down as chairman (lose a Board Room bonus temporarily), stock crash (-30%).
- High risk, high reward gambling. Maybe worth it if you cash out before SEC catches up.
- Real: Musk, $420, SEC lawsuit.

**Pivot to [Buzzword]**
- Announce the company is pivoting to AI/blockchain/metaverse/whatever.
- First pivot: big stock boost.
- Each subsequent pivot: less effective, more credibility damage.
- 3+ pivots: analysts downgrade, stock penalty.
- Real: every company adding "AI" to their name in 2023.

**Start a Side Project**
- CEO gets distracted by something unrelated.
- Options: tunnel boring company, social media platform, space rockets, crypto exchange
- Drains company resources (cash/tick).
- Small chance of massive payoff (rare jackpot).
- Mostly a money pit that entertains you.
- Real: Musk's entire portfolio.

**Tequila at the Board Meeting**
- Throw a corporate event that gets out of hand.
- Random outcome spectrum:
  - "Great team bonding" (+morale, +revenue)
  - "Questionable photos surface" (-stock)
  - "HR nightmare" (-stock, -cash for settlements)
- Real: Adam Neumann throwing bottles through windows, pot-filled jet flights.

**Write a Manifesto**
- Publish your "vision for the future of work."
- Either genius or cringe — random binary outcome.
- Genius: stock bump, +2-3 quarters of positive sentiment
- Cringe: stock dip, meme'd on social media, negative sentiment
- Stock is volatile for several days after regardless.
- Real: every LinkedIn thought leader CEO ever.

### 🏌️ THE "NETWORKING" (doing nothing productively)

**Golf with Investors**
- Costs time (long cooldown).
- Small chance of:
  - Landing a deal (cash bonus)
  - Favorable analyst coverage (+stock)
  - Nothing (most likely)
- Mostly just vibes.
- Real: the entire concept of executive golf.

**Davos / Conference Circuit**
- Expensive trip (cash cost).
- CEO is "unavailable" for other actions during the trip (locked out for N seconds).
- Small prestige bump, chance of good press.
- Chance of announcing something stupid on a panel (random stock penalty).
- Real: every Davos attendee.

**Charity Gala**
- Spend cash on philanthropy.
- Good PR (+stock), tax deduction (reduce quarterly tax).
- Diminishing returns — too much charity and activists call you a "philanthro-capitalist."
- Cynical version: charity is a stock manipulation tool. Which it kind of is.
- Real: every tech billionaire's foundation.

## Game Mechanics

### Stock Price — The Big Rework

**Current system (pre-CEO):**
```
valuation = totalAnnualRev × revenueMultiple
stockPrice = valuation / sharesOutstanding
```
Stock price is purely derived from revenue. No independent existence. No way to manipulate it separately from growing the business.

**CEO phase system: Decoupled stock price**

The core insight: **the gap between real value and perceived value IS the satire.** Real CEOs spend their careers inflating stock price above fundamental value. That's literally the game.

#### Fundamental Value (the truth)
```
fundamentalValue = totalAnnualRev × baseMultiple / sharesOutstanding
```
This ticks along automatically. Your automation chain (CTO/COO/CFO) drives revenue. The fundamental value is what the company is "actually worth." The player can't directly touch this anymore — they already built the machine.

#### Market Price (the perception)
```
marketPrice = fundamentalValue × sentimentMultiplier × momentumFactor
```

**Sentiment** is the CEO's playground. It's a floating multiplier (starts at 1.0) affected by:
- CEO actions (tweets, speeches, interviews push it up or down)
- Buybacks (directly inflate price above fundamentals)
- Scandals (sudden drops)
- Market mood (random drift)
- **Decay**: sentiment always regresses toward 1.0 over time. You have to keep pumping.

**Momentum** simulates market psychology:
- Positive momentum: when price is rising, it rises faster (FOMO)
- Negative momentum: when price is falling, it falls faster (panic selling)
- Creates natural boom/bust cycles the CEO has to surf

#### The Gap = The Danger
```
overvaluation = marketPrice / fundamentalValue
```
- **1.0-1.5×**: Healthy. Normal CEO optimism. No risk.
- **1.5-2.5×**: Stretched. Analyst skepticism events start firing. Small chance of correction.
- **2.5-4.0×**: Dangerous. SEC "informal inquiry." Media scrutiny. Increasing crash probability each tick.
- **4.0×+**: Bubble territory. Crash is almost inevitable. But if you cash out before it pops...

The overvaluation ratio is visible to the player as a subtle meter — maybe a "Market Confidence" gauge that goes from green to yellow to red to flashing.

#### Crash Mechanics
When overvaluation gets too high, each tick rolls against a crash probability:
```
crashChance = max(0, (overvaluation - 1.5) × 0.02) per tick
```
At 2.5× overvaluation: ~2% per tick. At 4.0×: ~5% per tick.

A crash snaps `sentimentMultiplier` back toward 1.0 (not instantly — it overcorrects to ~0.7-0.8, then recovers). The stock price tanks to BELOW fundamental value temporarily. Recovery takes many ticks.

This creates the core tension: **pump as high as you dare, cash out before the crash.**

#### Buyback Mechanics
Stock buybacks are special — they reduce `sharesOutstanding`, which directly inflates price per share without changing fundamentals. But:
- Costs real cash (taken from company coffers)
- Doesn't change revenue or fundamental value
- Makes future crashes hit harder (fewer shares = more volatile)
- Real companies spend $800B+/year on this

```
buyback(amount):
  sharesRetired = amount / marketPrice
  sharesOutstanding -= sharesRetired
  cash -= amount
  // Price goes up because same value ÷ fewer shares
```

#### Short Sellers
When overvaluation is high, short sellers appear. They:
- Apply downward pressure on sentiment each tick
- Can be fought with buybacks (but that costs cash)
- Go away if stock drops to near fundamental value
- Create a "tug of war" dynamic in the late CEO phase
- Real: Hindenburg Research, Citron, etc.

#### Analyst Ratings
3-5 fictional analysts with different personalities:
- **Bull analyst**: always positive, upgrades easily, downgrades slowly
- **Bear analyst**: skeptical, quick to downgrade on overvaluation
- **Momentum analyst**: follows the crowd, amplifies trends
- Each has a rating: Buy / Hold / Sell
- Net rating affects sentiment decay rate
- CEO actions (interviews, earnings calls) can target specific analysts

### Cooldowns
- Each action has a cooldown (some short, some long).
- Prevents spam, creates pacing.
- Some actions lock out others (Davos locks everything for its duration).

### Diminishing Returns
- Most actions get less effective with repetition.
- Prevents grinding any single action.
- Resets partially each quarter.

### Risk / Reward Tiers
- Safe actions: small impact, no downside (meetings, blog posts, WSJ).
- Moderate: meaningful impact, some variance (speeches, interviews, layoffs).
- High risk: huge potential swings (funding secured tweet, acquisitions, tequila).

### The Golden Parachute Loop
1. Enter CEO phase → market price is your score
2. CEO actions pump sentiment → stock rises above fundamentals
3. Self-dealing actions (raise, jet, office) add to golden parachute value
4. Golden parachute pays out at **market price**, not fundamental value
5. The incentive: inflate the stock as high as possible before cashing out
6. But: overvaluation increases crash risk. Cash out too late = crash = bad payout
7. "Cash Out" button always available — the question is WHEN
8. New game+ bonus = f(parachute value at exit, market price at exit)
9. Start over with multipliers → reach CEO phase faster → bigger pump → loop

**The core loop is a game of chicken.** How long do you ride the bubble?

### Prestige Payout Formula
```
prestigeMultiplier = 1 + log10(parachuteValue) × overvaluationBonus
overvaluationBonus = min(3.0, marketPrice / fundamentalValue)  // capped
```
- Cashing out at 1.0× overvaluation = safe but modest multiplier
- Cashing out at 3.0× = maximum bonus (if you can time it)
- Cashing out during/after a crash = overvaluation is back near 1.0 = you missed it
- **Rewards skillful timing, not just patience**

## Open Questions

1. **How expensive should the CEO hire be?** It's the final Board Room purchase. Needs to feel like an achievement, not something you stumble into. 50,000 RE? 100,000 RE?

2. **Is the CEO phase reversible?** Current thinking: NO. It's a one-way door. The only way "back" is to cash out and start over. This makes the decision weighty.

3. **How long should the CEO phase sustain?** Target: 15-20 minutes of meaningful play before the player naturally wants to cash out. Enough actions and variety to explore, but not so much that it becomes its own game.

4. **Should the stock price graph be visible?** A real-time stock chart showing the impact of your actions could be very satisfying. Line goes up = dopamine. Line goes down = panic tweet. Could show both market price (green/red line) and fundamental value (gray dashed line) — the gap between them IS the game.

5. **Multiple prestige layers?** First prestige = CEO who cashes out. Second prestige = "Serial Entrepreneur" who starts a new company? Third = "Venture Capitalist" who invests in other companies? Each layer could be a different mini-game. Long-term thinking.

6. **Board resistance?** Should the board ever block CEO actions? (e.g., "The board rejected your proposal for a fourth corporate jet.") Adds friction but also comedy. Could tie to overvaluation — board gets nervous at high ratios.

7. **SEC / regulatory risk?** Current design has overvaluation naturally increasing crash risk. Should there ALSO be explicit SEC investigation events triggered by specific actions (Funding Secured tweet, Cooking the Books)? Separate from the crash mechanic — a targeted penalty for specific bad behavior.

8. **How does revenue keep flowing?** All the automation from pre-CEO phase keeps running. CTO/COO/CFO/VP of Ops all continue working. Revenue grows passively. CEO actions modulate sentiment/stock price, which is decoupled from revenue. The company runs itself — you're just the face.

9. **Should the player see the crash probability?** Showing "Crash Risk: 3.2%/day" makes it a pure math decision. Hiding it makes it feel more like gambling. Maybe: show a qualitative indicator ("Market Confidence: Fragile") without exact numbers?

10. **Earnings still happen in CEO phase?** Quarterly earnings could cause sentiment shocks — beat expectations = sentiment boost, miss = sentiment crash. But expectations are now set by the inflated stock price, not fundamentals. So high overvaluation makes earnings misses more likely. Another pressure to cash out.
